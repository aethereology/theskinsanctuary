# design.md

## Executive summary

Build The Skin Sanctuary Co as a warm, editorial, boutique esthetics website for Chesapeake, Virginia, with one dominant conversion goal: get the visitor to book through Square. The visual tone should feel calm, polished, feminine, welcoming, and lightly luxurious without becoming overly clinical or overly trendy.

This should not feel like a generic spa website. It should feel like Monica’s space: intimate, detail-oriented, relaxed, and result-aware. The strongest internal differentiators from the supplied materials are:
- a highly welcoming client experience
- strong client trust and repeat intent
- Korean lash lift and tint as a standout service
- a live, entry-level lead offer: the $35 Welcome Facial
- a simple, clean current brand expression with warm neutrals and a dark booking button

## Brand positioning

**Core positioning:**  
Personalized facials, lashes, brows, and waxing in a welcoming Chesapeake studio.

**Brand promise:**  
A calm beauty space where clients feel cared for, comfortable, and intentionally polished.

**Voice:**  
Warm, polished, low-pressure, encouraging, detail-oriented, lightly luxurious, never sterile.

**Do say:**  
glow, refreshed, tailored, polished, comfortable, welcoming, personalized, low-maintenance beauty, confidence, sanctuary

**Do not say:**  
aggressive “medical spa” language, exaggerated transformation claims, trendy slang overload, jargon-heavy skincare copy, fake luxury clichés

## Global content strategy

### Homepage intent
Immediate conversion and trust: this page must answer who Monica is, where the studio is, what she offers, why clients trust her, and how to book.

Remove booking hesitation by showing clear prices, durations, and a clean category structure.

### Welcome Facial intent
Capture first-time visitors from Instagram, Google, Linktree, and referral traffic with a low-commitment offer and simple CTA path.

### About page intent
Turn Monica from “a provider name” into a trusted person with a clear care philosophy.

### Reviews page intent
Stack proof and reduce doubt.

### Contact page intent
Make directions, hours, and booking frictionless.

### Policies page intent
Set expectations and protect the schedule without sounding harsh.

## Photography direction

### Overall approach
Use real, warm, clean studio imagery. Avoid generic stock whenever possible.

### Image priorities
1. Monica portrait
2. Treatment-room environmental shot
3. Facial treatment in progress
4. Korean lash lift close-up result
5. Brow lamination / tint close-up
6. Brow wax result
7. Facial glow-after portrait
8. Flat-lay or detail shot of tools / linens / ambiance
9. Exterior or arrival cue
10. One image specific to the Welcome Facial promo
11. Additional client-results images for gallery strip
12. Optional Google-review user photos if rights/quality allow

### Framing notes
- Favor vertical crops for mobile and portrait modules.
- Favor close crops for lashes, brows, skin texture, and facial massage details.
- Keep waxing imagery tasteful and non-explicit.
- Use one “space” image per page cluster to keep the site from feeling all close-ups.

### Editing notes
- Keep tones warm and neutral.
- Avoid oversmoothing skin.
- Preserve realistic texture.
- Maintain consistent white balance across the set.

### Type scale
- H1: 48–72 px desktop, 36–44 px mobile
- H2: 32–44 px desktop, 28–34 px mobile
- H3: 22–28 px
- Body: 16–18 px
- Fine print: 14 px minimum

## Accessibility notes

- Maintain strong contrast for all body text and buttons.
- Do not place taupe text on oat backgrounds unless contrast is tested.
- Use visible focus states that fit the brand.
- Make every CTA keyboard reachable.
- Ensure all interactive elements meet minimum touch-target expectations.
- Decorative photos or flourishes should use empty alt text.
- Informative photos should have concise, purpose-based alt text.
- Service tables must remain readable on mobile; convert to cards or stacked rows if needed.
- Avoid autoplay motion.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Use one H1 per page.
- Ensure accordions and menus announce expanded/collapsed states.

## Responsive layout specs

### Breakpoints
- Mobile: 375–767 px
- Tablet: 768–1023 px
- Desktop: 1024–1439 px
- Wide desktop: 1440 px+

### Grid
- Mobile: 4-column conceptual grid, 16 px gutters
- Tablet: 8-column grid, 24 px gutters
- Desktop: 12-column grid, 24–32 px gutters
- Max content width: 1200–1280 px

### Spacing system
Base spacing unit: 8 px  
Primary section padding:
- Mobile: 56–72 px
- Tablet: 72–88 px
- Desktop: 88–120 px

### Homepage responsive rules
- Hero image stacks below text on mobile
- Trust strip becomes 2 columns on mobile
- Services become 1 column on mobile, 2 on tablet, 4 on desktop
- Review cards become swipeable on small screens
- Service pricing tables collapse to stacked cards below 768 px

## Sticky booking CTA behavior

### Desktop
- Header is sticky after scroll.
- Primary Book Now button remains visible at all times.
- On service pages, add a small secondary sticky utility row with “Book Now” and “Call / Text” only after the hero leaves the viewport.

### Mobile
- Persistent bottom bar appears after first scroll.
- Primary action: Book Now
- Secondary action: Call / Text
- On the Welcome Facial page, primary action label changes to “Book $35 Facial”
- Respect safe-area inset on iPhone devices
- Keep bar height compact and thumb-friendly
